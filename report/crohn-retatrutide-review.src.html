§0Resumen ejecutivo

Este informe revisa la evidencia sobre retatrutida 4 mg semanales en una paciente con enfermedad de Crohn. La conclusión central cambió respecto al borrador anterior.

Lo nuevo. La dosis de 4 mg ya no depende de extrapolar desde el ensayo de fase 2: tiene su propio brazo en el ensayo de fase 3 TRIUMPH-1 (n = 2.339, 80 semanas), con una pérdida media de peso del 19,0 %. Eso es eficacia sustancial y bien documentada.

La corrección importante. El borrador anterior afirmaba que los efectos gastrointestinales son «sustancialmente menores» a 4 mg. Los datos por dosis lo desmienten: la diarrea aparece en el 25,2 % a 4 mg frente al 32,0 % a 12 mg, y el estreñimiento en el 23,8 % frente al 26,1 %. La reducción es modesta, no sustancial. En una paciente con Crohn esto significa que una de cada cuatro semanas de diarrea puede ser del fármaco y no del brote, y los síntomas por sí solos no permiten distinguirlos. Ese es el problema clínico que organiza todo el protocolo.

La segunda corrección. El borrador atribuía a los agonistas GLP-1 una reducción del íleo (aHR 0,58) y de las cirugías de Crohn (aHR 0,55) citando un artículo de Frontiers in Immunology con 167.000 pacientes. No se sostiene. La revista correcta es Frontiers in Medicine, las cohortes reunidas suman unos 16.242 pacientes, la reducción de cirugía es HR 0,61 (IC 95 % 0,44–0,84), y sobre el íleo no hay asociación significativa en ninguna dirección. El mensaje honesto es: tranquilizador en seguridad, no demostrado en beneficio.

Lo que realmente hay que vigilar. No es la inflamación —es la masa magra y el estado nutricional. Entre el 15 % y el 40 % del peso perdido con incretinas es tejido magro, y en Crohn se suma la malabsorción ileal. Las tres palancas son proteína (1,6–2,2 g/kg/día), fuerza tres veces por semana, y hierro, B12 y vitamina D medidos, no supuestos.

Sobre los péptidos añadidos. KPV y BPC-157 tienen datos únicamente en roedores; TB-500 no tiene datos humanos de eficacia de ningún tipo. Eso no los invalida como hipótesis, pero sí los sitúa varios escalones por debajo de la retatrutida en la escala de evidencia (figura 4), y conviene decirlo con esa claridad antes de añadir un tercer o cuarto compuesto no aprobado a un intestino inflamado.

Advertencia sobre el origen del producto

Todas las cifras de este informe proceden de retatrutida de grado farmacéutico administrada en ensayos clínicos. Un vial de «uso en investigación» del mercado gris no tiene contenido, pureza ni esterilidad verificados. Si la concentración real difiere de la etiquetada, la dosis administrada no es 4 mg — y entonces ninguna de las curvas dosis-respuesta de este documento describe lo que está ocurriendo. Es la limitación más grande del caso y no se resuelve con monitorización.

How to read the evidence tags

Every substantive claim carries a tag showing what kind of study stands behind it. The tag is the point: two claims can read identically in prose and rest on completely different evidence.

RCT
Randomised controlled trial in humans.
Cohort
Observational human data. Shows association; confounding by indication is rarely excluded.
Guideline
Formal multi-society clinical guidance.
Animal
Preclinical only. No human efficacy data exist.
No data
Reasoning from mechanism alone. Treat as hypothesis.
Mechanism
Established physiology, applied by inference to this case.

§2Retatrutide at 4 mg: pharmacology

Retatrutide (LY3437943, Eli Lilly) is a once-weekly subcutaneous single-peptide triple agonist at the GLP-1, GIP and glucagon receptors — the addition of glucagon agonism is what separates it from semaglutide and tirzepatide.RCT

GIP Biased signalling; augments the anorexigenic effect and appears to improve GI tolerability relative to GLP-1 alone.Mechanism Neutral to favourable. Likely part of why nausea, though common, is mostly mild to moderate.

§3Efficacy and tolerability at 4 mg

3.1 The dose now has its own randomised read-out

TRIUMPH-1 randomised 2,339 adults 1:1:1:1 to retatrutide 4, 9 or 12 mg or placebo for 80 weeks, starting at 2 mg weekly with escalation every 4 weeks. The cohort was severe: mean BMI 40.0 kg/m², mean baseline weight 112.7 kg. Mean weight reduction was 19.0% at 4 mg, 25.9% at 9 mg and 28.3% at 12 mg; 45.3% of the 12 mg group lost ≥30% of body weight, and a 104-week extension in participants with baseline BMI ≥35 reached a mean of 30.3%.RCT

3.2 Tolerability: where the previous draft went wrong

Adverse events were dose-related, predominantly gastrointestinal, and mostly mild to moderate. But "dose-related" was doing a lot of unexamined work in the earlier draft. Broken out by dose, the gradient is steep for nausea and vomiting and shallow for diarrhoea and constipation — and it is diarrhoea that matters most in Crohn's disease.RCT

The finding that reorganises the protocol

At 4 mg, retatrutide is well tolerated in aggregate — discontinuation is no higher than placebo. But it produces diarrhoea in about a quarter of users and constipation in about a quarter. In a patient whose disease presents with exactly those symptoms, the drug installs a permanent source of diagnostic noise. Every subsequent decision in this document — the marker schedule, the seven-day rule, the algorithm in §7 — exists to cut through that noise. Symptom diaries alone cannot do it.

3.3 Body composition: the unmeasured risk

Across incretin trials, lean tissue accounts for roughly 15–40% of total weight lost: about 25% in SURMOUNT-1 (tirzepatide) and closer to 45% in STEP 1 (semaglutide), where lean mass fell 9.7% against a 19.3% fall in fat mass.RCT No body-composition data have been published for retatrutide at these doses, and its glucagon arm makes extrapolation from the other agents less safe rather than more.No data

At an expected 19% total weight loss, the plausible lean-mass loss for this patient spans roughly 3–8% of body weight — on top of the sarcopenia risk that active Crohn's disease already carries. The levers that shift the ratio back toward fat are established and few: resistance training and protein intake. Reported protein intakes in cohorts that preserved lean tissue were 1.6–2.3 g/kg fat-free mass per day with resistance training 3–5 days per week.Cohort This is the single most actionable finding in the report and it is developed in §9.

§4GLP-1 receptor agonists in inflammatory bowel disease

This is where the previous draft was least reliable, so the standard of proof here is deliberately strict. The honest summary: reassuring on safety, unproven on benefit.

4.1 What is actually established

  • No signal of harm. Across multiple datasets, GLP-1 RA use was not associated with increased IBD exacerbations. Given how plausible a mechanistic worry it was, this is the most useful finding for a patient already taking one.Cohort
  • Surgery. Pooled HR 0.61 (95% CI 0.44–0.84) across 11 studies and 16,242 patients — a real association, and the strongest positive signal in the literature.Cohort
  • Hospitalisation. HR 0.91 (0.71–1.17) overall, 0.93 (0.78–1.10) in Crohn's, 0.88 (0.66–1.18) in ulcerative colitis. All cross 1.0.Cohort
  • Ileus and obstruction. No significant association in either direction; roughly 1–2% of patients experienced such an event.Cohort
  • Steroid use. Several large registries report reduced corticosteroid exposure among users, though pooled estimates are not consistent.Cohort
  • Weight and metabolic outcomes. Patients with IBD and obesity achieve substantial weight loss, comparable to non-IBD populations.Cohort

4.2 Why these numbers deserve caution

All of the above is retrospective. Three limitations bear directly on how much weight to put on them:

  • Confounding by indication. Patients prescribed a GLP-1 RA are, almost by construction, those whose disease is quiescent enough for a clinician to prioritise weight. Comparing them to non-users compares two different diseases in two different states.
  • Heterogeneity. One meta-analysis returned an IBD-related surgery odds ratio of 0.32 with a confidence interval of 0.00 to 313.88 and I² = 92.5% — an interval so wide it carries no information, and a useful reminder of how unstable this literature still is.
  • No retatrutide data at all. Every estimate above comes from semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide and tirzepatide. Retatrutide's glucagon receptor agonism has no analogue in that evidence base. Extending a class effect to it is inference, not observation.No data
How to state this to a gastroenterologist

"The class shows no increase in IBD exacerbations across large cohorts, and an association with reduced surgery (HR 0.61). Hospitalisation and obstruction outcomes are neutral. There is no randomised IBD evidence, and none at all for retatrutide specifically." That framing is defensible in a clinic conversation. "GLP-1s reduce Crohn's inflammation" is not.

§6Baseline workup and disease mechanism

6.1 Why chronic pain in Crohn's is rarely one thing

Persistent abdominal pain in Crohn's disease usually combines three separable processes, and they call for different responses:Mechanism

  1. Active transmural inflammation — the Th1/Th17 axis driving TNF-α and the IL-23 pathway. Responds to disease-modifying therapy; tracks with calprotectin and CRP.
  2. Mechanical distension from stricture or partial obstruction. Does not track with inflammatory markers, and is the process most plausibly aggravated by delayed transit.
  3. Visceral hypersensitivity and central sensitisation, amplified by IL-1β and IL-6 acting on the CNS. Persists after mucosal healing and is frequently mistaken for treatment failure.

In a 40-year-old woman, three further contributors deserve explicit exclusion: iron and B12 deficiency from terminal ileal disease, osteopenia from cumulative corticosteroid exposure, and coexisting endometriosis, whose cyclical pain is routinely attributed to bowel disease for years.

6.2 Immunology, briefly

Crohn's disease is a dysregulated innate immune response plus an exaggerated Th1/Th17 response to commensal microbiota in a genetically susceptible host (NOD2, ATG16L1, IL23R). Hyperactivated macrophages and dendritic cells oversecrete TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-12 and IL-23; IFN-γ and IL-17 sustain mucosal injury; a damaged epithelial barrier admits bacterial antigen to gut-associated lymphoid tissue, closing a self-sustaining loop. The IL-23/Th17 axis is the target of ustekinumab and risankizumab.Mechanism

Faecal calprotectin remains the reference non-invasive marker of mucosal inflammation, and CRP the systemic one. Their value in this case is not diagnostic novelty — it is that they are the only instruments that can separate drug effect from disease activity when the symptoms are identical.

§7Flare or side effect: the decision algorithm

Given a 25% background rate of drug-induced diarrhoea, the question "is this the disease or the drug?" will arise repeatedly. Answering it by impression is unreliable; the rule below makes it mechanical.

7.1 The rule, stated plainly

Any sustained change in GI symptoms lasting more than seven days triggers faecal calprotectin and CRP before it is attributed to anything. Not a diary entry, not a dose adjustment, not reassurance — a measurement. Two failure modes this prevents, in both directions:

  • Attributing a flare to the drug delays escalation of disease-modifying therapy while transmural inflammation continues. This is the more dangerous error.
  • Attributing a drug effect to a flare triggers unnecessary steroids, imaging and anxiety, and may cost her a therapy that is working metabolically.

These are obstruction, abscess and perforation questions. They are not titration problems, and no monitoring schedule in this document should delay acting on them.

Built on the Crohn's Disease Exclusion Diet, which has randomised evidence for inducing remission,RCT and then adapted for the specific problem that appetite is now pharmacologically suppressed.

8.1 Rules that change because of the drug

  • Protein first at every meal. Satiety arrives early and unpredictably. Whatever is eaten first is what actually gets eaten — so it should be the protein.
  • Five to six small meals. Large meals worsen Crohn's pain and incretin nausea by the same mechanism. This is the rare instance where both problems have one answer.
  • Liquid protein as a floor, not a treat. On low-appetite days a shake is often the only realistic route to the target. Plan for it rather than treating it as failure.
  • Everything cooked in phase 1, chewed thoroughly, with 2–2.5 L of fluid daily — which also addresses the 24% constipation rate.
  • Avoid NSAIDs. Unchanged, and independent of the drug.

§9Training and body composition

Moderate exercise is anti-inflammatory through muscle-derived IL-6 and regulatory T-cell modulation; high-volume, high-intensity work raises TNF-α and IL-1β and is a plausible flare trigger.Cohort On a triple agonist, resistance training moves from beneficial to load-bearing: it is one of only two levers protecting lean tissue.

Measure the right thing

Scale weight will fall on this drug regardless of what the training does — which makes it useless as feedback. Track instead: waist circumference, a repeatable strength benchmark (a fixed load for maximum reps), grip strength, and DXA lean mass if accessible. If strength is holding while weight falls, the protocol is working. If strength falls with weight, protein and training are losing and the plan needs to change, not the dose.

10.1 A defensible sequence

Adding two or three unapproved compounds simultaneously to an inflamed gut makes attribution impossible: if something improves or worsens, nothing will indicate which agent did it. If adjuncts are pursued at all, sequence them and measure between steps.

  1. Weeks 0–8 — change nothing pharmacological. Diet phase 1, the protein floor, three weekly training sessions, sleep. Hold retatrutide at 4 mg. Measure calprotectin and CRP at week 8. Most of the achievable benefit lives in this step, and it is the only one with no added risk.
  2. If markers remain elevated and the gastroenterologist agrees: introduce a single agent — KPV has the most relevant preclinical rationale — for 6–8 weeks, with markers before and after.
  3. Only then, and only if weight and body-composition goals justify it while GI symptoms are controlled and stenosis excluded, consider discussing escalation from 4 mg.

Stop any adjunct immediately for pain above 3/10, bleeding, fever, injection-site infection or rapid unintended weight loss.

The honest position on the adjuncts

KPV, BPC-157 and TB-500 are unapproved research chemicals. The relevant risk is not only that they may not work — it is that grey-market material carries unverified identity, purity, endotoxin load and sterility, injected by a patient who is immunosuppressed. That combination is the specific hazard, and it is not mitigated by careful dosing or by monitoring. It can only be mitigated by not doing it, or by doing it under clinical supervision with material of verified provenance.

§11Monitoring schedule

Table 12. Rolling eight-week cycle at a stable 4 mg dose. The cycle restarts on any dose change.
WeekActionsDecision it informs
0 Full baseline panel (Table 6). HBI. Weight, waist, photographs, strength benchmark. Confirm stenosis status. Begin diet phase 1. Hold retatrutide dose constant. Establishes the reference point. Without it, nothing later is interpretable.
2 Review GI symptoms against Table 7. Audit actual protein intake for three days — measured, not estimated. Confirm training is stable at RPE 4–6. Early detection of an intake shortfall, which is the most common and most correctable failure.
4 FBC and LFTs if on a thiopurine. Weight and waist. Formal flare-versus-adverse-effect review. Catches absorption-related haematological drift and consolidates the symptom picture.
8 Faecal calprotectin, CRP, ferritin and transferrin saturation, B12, folate, 25-OH-D, HbA1c, lipids, albumin. Weight, waist, strength, pain score, sleep quality. The decision point. Markers quiet and strength holding → continue unchanged. Markers rising → gastroenterology, not adjuncts. Strength falling → fix protein and training before touching the dose.
Interpreting the week-8 panel

The panel answers three questions in order of priority. Is the disease active? Calprotectin and CRP. Is she becoming depleted? Ferritin, B12, folate, vitamin D, albumin. Is she losing the right tissue? Strength benchmark against weight change. A good result on weight with a bad result on any of the other three is not a good result.

§12Ten priorities, ranked

Table 13. Ordered by consequence if neglected, not by how interesting they are.
#PriorityWhy it ranks hereConcrete action
1Separating flare from side effect A 25% drug-induced diarrhoea rate means symptoms have permanently lost their diagnostic value. Getting this wrong in either direction is the highest-consequence error available. The seven-day rule (§7). Calprotectin and CRP before attribution, every time.
2Defending lean mass 15–40% of the ~19% expected weight loss can be lean tissue, on top of existing sarcopenia risk. Once lost it is slow and difficult to regain. Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day and three weekly resistance sessions. Track strength, not weight.
3Product provenance If the vial does not contain what the label says, every number in this report describes a different drug than the one being injected. Verified supply and clinical supervision, or an approved alternative (§10).
4Stenosis status Determines whether delayed transit is a nuisance or a hazard, and gates any dose escalation. Current intestinal ultrasound or MR enterography before any change.
5Micronutrient depletion Suppressed intake plus ileal malabsorption compound. Fatigue from anaemia is routinely misread as disease activity. Ferritin >50 ng/mL, plus B12, folate and vitamin D repleted and re-measured at eight weeks.
6Gastroenterology co-management An unapproved triple agonist alongside biologics or thiopurines is not a self-managed situation. Disclose the drug and dose. Ask specifically about stenosis and thiopurine monitoring.
7Training dose Moderate loading is anti-inflammatory; excessive loading is a flare trigger. The window is real and narrow. RPE 4–6, deload every fourth week, stop if pain exceeds 2/10.
8Sleep and stress Short sleep raises IL-6 and TNF-α; stress is among the best-documented relapse triggers. Cheap to fix, easy to ignore. 7–9 h sleep; ten minutes daily of breathing work or a CBT-based practice.
9Bone health Steroid history, chronic inflammation and rapid weight loss all reduce bone density, silently, at 40. DXA. Load-bearing resistance work. Vitamin D, K2 and calcium adequacy.
10Adjunct peptide sequencing Ranks last deliberately: the weakest evidence, and adding several at once destroys attribution. One agent at a time, markers either side, only after the eight-week foundation block.

You are a multidisciplinary team: an IBD gastroenterologist, an immunologist, a CDED-certified
clinical dietitian, an exercise physiologist and a peptide pharmacologist. Produce a rigorously
cited report (PubMed, NEJM, Lancet, Gut, J Crohns Colitis; last 5 years) on a case: a
40-year-old woman with ileal Crohn's disease and chronic abdominal pain, self-administering
INVESTIGATIONAL RETATRUTIDE 4 mg subcutaneously once weekly.

Verify each of the following before using it; correct anything that does not hold up, and say so.

1. PHARMACOLOGY FIRST. Retatrutide (LY3437943): GLP-1 + GIP + glucagon triple agonism with
   biased GIP signalling; half-life approximately 6 days; approximately dose-proportional
   exposure. Efficacy: phase 2 (Jastreboff, NEJM 2023;389:514-526), 48-week least-squares mean
   weight change -8.7% (1 mg), -17.1% (4 mg combined), -22.8% (8 mg combined), -24.2% (12 mg),
   -2.1% placebo. Phase 3 TRIUMPH-1: n=2,339, 1:1:1:1, 80 weeks, 2 mg start with 4-weekly
   escalation, mean baseline BMI 40.0 kg/m2 and weight 112.7 kg; -19.0% (4 mg), -25.9% (9 mg),
   -28.3% (12 mg); 45.3% achieved >=30% at 12 mg; up to -30.3% at 104 weeks in a BMI>=35
   extension.

2. DOSE-STRATIFIED TOLERABILITY, which is the clinical crux. TRIUMPH-1, 4 / 9 / 12 mg vs
   placebo: nausea 28.6 / 38.4 / 42.4 vs 14.8%; diarrhoea 25.2 / 34.1 / 32.0 vs 13.5%;
   constipation 23.8 / 25.9 / 26.1 vs 10.9%; vomiting 10.6 / 22.8 / 25.3 vs 4.8%; dysaesthesia
   5.1 / 12.3 / 12.5 vs 0.9%; discontinuation for AEs 4.1 / 6.9 / 11.3 vs 4.9%. Analyse
   explicitly what a ~25% drug-induced diarrhoea rate does to symptom-based assessment of
   Crohn's disease activity.

3. GLP-1 CLASS EVIDENCE IN IBD, stated without overreach. Frontiers in Medicine
   2025;12:1621958; J Crohns Colitis 2025;19(10):jjaf181, 19(9):jjaf167, 19(12):jjaf193.
   Surgery pooled HR 0.61 (0.44-0.84), 11 studies, 16,242 patients; hospitalisation HR 0.91
   (0.71-1.17), Crohn's 0.93 (0.78-1.10), UC 0.88 (0.66-1.18); no significant association with
   ileus or intestinal obstruction; no increase in IBD exacerbations. State the confounding-by-
   indication problem and that no RCT has enrolled an IBD population, and none exists for
   retatrutide.

4. INTERACTIONS AND PERIOPERATIVE CARE. Biologics, thiopurines (delayed gastric emptying and
   oral absorption kinetics), stricturing disease. 2024 multisociety perioperative guidance
   (AGA, ASMBS, ASA, ISPCOP, SAGES, 29 Oct 2024).

5. BODY COMPOSITION. Lean mass as a fraction of incretin-induced weight loss (~25% in
   SURMOUNT-1, ~45% in STEP 1, ~15-40% across reviews); note the absence of retatrutide
   body-composition data and why its glucagon arm makes extrapolation unsafe. Protein targets
   1.6-2.3 g/kg fat-free mass; resistance training 3-5 days/week.

6. NUTRITION. CDED protocol (Levine, Gastroenterology 2019;157:440-450) adapted for
   pharmacological appetite suppression: protein-first meals, 5-6 small meals, liquid protein
   as a floor. Grade every supplement by evidence level.

7. ADJUNCT PEPTIDES. KPV (Kannengiesser, Inflamm Bowel Dis 2008;14:324-331 - murine only),

   authorisation), TB-500 (no human efficacy data). Give mechanism, published research ranges,
   interaction considerations with biologics and thiopurines, and regulatory status. Address
   grey-market provenance risk in an immunosuppressed patient.

8. DELIVERABLES. A flare-versus-adverse-effect decision algorithm; an 8-week monitoring
